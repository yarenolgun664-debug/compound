Frequently Asked Questions

Compound interest is the addition of interest to the principal sum of a deposit or loan. In simple terms, it is earning "interest on interest." By reinvesting your profits rather than withdrawing them, you generate return cycles on both the initial principal and the accumulated wealth over time.
Simple interest is calculated solely as a percentage of the initial principal balance, paid out statically every cycle. Compound interest calculates interest on the growing total balance (principal + prior interest). This creates a compounding effect that triggers exponential wealth curves.
Historically, US stock indexes (like the S&P 500) have returned a compound annual growth rate of roughly 9% to 10% over long periods before inflation. Cash savings profiles (HYSA) yield around 4% to 5%. For conservative projections, investors often assume a return rate of 6% to 8%.
The Rule of 72 is a simplified math formula used to estimate how many years it will take for an investment to double in value at a fixed annual yield. Simply divide 72 by your annual interest rate to find the approximate doubling time (e.g. at a 7.2% yield, your money doubles in about 10 years).
